## 1. Data Protection and GDPR Compliance

### 1.1 Scope and Legal Basis

Meridian Group processes personal data in the course of its business activities, including employee records, customer information, patient data (Healthcare segment), and supplier details. All processing of personal data must comply with the UK General Data Protection Regulation (UK GDPR), the EU General Data Protection Regulation (EU GDPR 2016/679), and applicable national data protection laws.

Processing personal data is only permitted where a valid lawful basis exists. The six lawful bases are: consent, contract, legal obligation, vital interests, public task, and legitimate interests. The Processing Register must be updated whenever a new category of personal data is processed or an existing processing purpose changes.

### 1.2 Data Subject Rights

Data subjects have the following rights under the GDPR, which Meridian must fulfil within statutory timeframes:

- **Right of Access:** Respond to Subject Access Requests (SARs) within 30 calendar days.
- **Right to Erasure ("Right to be Forgotten"):** Erase personal data when there is no longer a legitimate basis for processing.
- **Right to Data Portability:** Provide personal data in a structured, machine-readable format upon request.
- **Right to Restriction:** Restrict processing while a data subject contests accuracy or objects to processing.
- **Right to Object:** Honour objections to direct marketing processing immediately.

All data subject requests must be logged in the Rights Management System and escalated to the Data Protection team within 2 business days of receipt.

### 1.3 Data Breach Notification

In the event of a personal data breach, the following procedure applies:

1. Identify and contain the breach immediately.
2. Notify the Data Protection Officer (DPO) within **4 hours** of discovery.
3. Assess the risk to data subjects' rights and freedoms.
4. If the breach is likely to result in a risk to individuals, notify the relevant supervisory authority (ICO in the UK; lead supervisory authority in the EU) **within 72 hours** of becoming aware of the breach.
5. If the breach is likely to result in a **high risk** to individuals, notify affected data subjects without undue delay.
6. Document all breaches in the Breach Register, regardless of whether notification is required.

Failure to notify within the 72-hour window may result in regulatory sanctions.

### 1.4 Data Protection Officer

Meridian has appointed **Helen Forsythe** as Group Data Protection Officer (DPO). The DPO is an independent function reporting directly to the General Counsel. Contact: dpo@meridiangroup.com.

The DPO must be consulted on all matters relating to data protection, including high-risk processing activities, new product development involving personal data, and responses to regulatory enquiries.

### 1.5 Data Protection Impact Assessments (DPIA)

A Data Protection Impact Assessment (DPIA) is mandatory before commencing any processing that is likely to result in a high risk to individuals. Processing activities that require a DPIA include:

- Systematic and extensive profiling with significant effects on individuals
- Large-scale processing of special category data (health, biometric, genetic data)
- Systematic monitoring of publicly accessible areas
- Any new use of artificial intelligence to make automated decisions about individuals

DPIAs must be completed using the Group's DPIA template and approved by the DPO before processing commences. If residual risks cannot be mitigated, the supervisory authority must be consulted prior to processing.

### 1.6 Fines and Penalties

Breaches of the GDPR can result in administrative fines of up to **€20 million**, or in the case of undertakings, **4% of the total worldwide annual turnover** of the preceding financial year, whichever is higher (Tier 2 infringements). Less serious infringements may attract fines of up to €10 million or 2% of global annual turnover (Tier 1).

## 2. Information Security Policy

### 2.1 Governance and Standards

Meridian maintains an Information Security Management System (ISMS) certified to **ISO 27001:2022**. The ISMS covers all Group operations globally. The Chief Information Security Officer (CISO) is **Michael Tran**, who reports to the Group Chief Technology Officer.

The Information Security Steering Committee meets quarterly to review the threat landscape, approve security investments, and track remediation of control deficiencies.

### 2.2 Data Classification

All Meridian data assets must be classified according to the following scheme:

| Classification | Description | Examples |
|----------------|-------------|---------|
| **Public** | Approved for external distribution | Press releases, published reports |
| **Internal** | For employee use only | Internal procedures, org charts |
| **Confidential** | Sensitive business information | Financial forecasts, contracts, HR data |
| **Restricted** | Highest sensitivity, strictly controlled | M&A plans, patient data, cryptographic keys |

Restricted data must be encrypted at rest (AES-256) and in transit (TLS 1.3 minimum) at all times.

### 2.3 Access Control and Authentication

**Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA)** is mandatory for all employees accessing any Meridian systems, effective from Q1 2024. MFA is enforced via Microsoft Entra ID. Access requests must be approved by the employee's line manager and the system owner before provisioning.

Password requirements: minimum **12 characters**, must include uppercase, lowercase, numbers, and a special character. Passwords must be changed every **90 days**. Password reuse for the previous 10 passwords is prohibited. Privileged accounts (admin/service accounts) must use 20-character passwords and are subject to quarterly review.

### 2.4 Security Incident Reporting

All suspected or confirmed security incidents must be reported to the Security Operations Centre (SOC) via the incident portal (security.meridiangroup.com) or by calling the 24/7 security hotline (+44 800 SEC-0001). Employees must report incidents **within 24 hours** of discovery.

The SOC will triage incidents within 1 hour and escalate to the CISO for incidents classified as Severity 1 (critical) or Severity 2 (high). The CISO will notify the CEO and General Counsel for incidents with potential regulatory, reputational, or financial impact exceeding $1 million.

Annual **penetration testing** is conducted by an independent third party. Results are reviewed by the CISO and presented to the Audit Committee. Critical vulnerabilities must be remediated within 30 days; high vulnerabilities within 90 days.

## 3. Financial Compliance

### 3.1 Record Retention

All financial records, including general ledger entries, invoices, contracts, bank statements, payroll records, tax filings, and audit documentation, must be retained for a **minimum of 7 years** from the end of the financial year to which they relate, in compliance with applicable tax laws and accounting standards.

Records involving litigation, regulatory investigations, or unresolved disputes must be placed on Legal Hold and retained indefinitely until the matter is resolved. The Group Archivist (archives@meridiangroup.com) manages the Records Management System.

### 3.2 Sarbanes-Oxley (SOX) Compliance

Meridian's US-listed entities are subject to the Sarbanes-Oxley Act of 2002. The Group maintains documented internal controls over financial reporting (ICFR) in accordance with the COSO 2013 framework. Key controls are tested annually by both internal audit and the external auditors.

Management's Assessment of ICFR is signed by the **Chief Financial Officer Rebecca Sung** and the CEO prior to filing with the SEC. No material weaknesses in internal controls were identified during FY2023.

### 3.3 Anti-Money Laundering (AML) and Sanctions

All new business relationships must be subject to Customer Due Diligence (CDD) screening before onboarding. Enhanced Due Diligence (EDD) is required for Politically Exposed Persons (PEPs) and entities in high-risk jurisdictions.

Sanctions screening must be conducted against the following lists: US OFAC SDN list, UN Consolidated Sanctions list, EU Consolidated Sanctions list, and HM Treasury UK Consolidated Sanctions list. Screening is performed at onboarding and re-screened monthly thereafter.

Suspicious Activity Reports (SARs) must be filed with the relevant Financial Intelligence Unit (FinCEN in the US; NCA in the UK) when there are grounds to suspect money laundering or terrorist financing. SARs are filed by the Group Money Laundering Reporting Officer (MLRO): **Adrian Holloway**.

### 3.4 Quarterly Financial Reporting

Quarterly financial results must be prepared and filed with regulators within **45 days** of the quarter-end. The reporting timetable is approved by the CFO in January each year. All material disclosures are reviewed by external legal counsel before filing.

## 4. Whistleblowing Policy

### 4.1 Purpose and Scope

Meridian is committed to a culture of transparency and ethical conduct. All employees, contractors, suppliers, and other stakeholders are encouraged to report concerns about actual or suspected wrongdoing, including fraud, financial misconduct, regulatory breaches, health and safety violations, environmental damage, and other unethical behaviour.

### 4.2 Reporting Channels

Concerns can be reported through the following channels:

- **EthicsLine (anonymous, 24/7):** Call **0800-ETHICS (0800-384427)** or visit ethics.meridiangroup.com
- **Email:** ethics@meridiangroup.com (monitored by the Compliance team)
- **In writing:** Chief Compliance Officer, Meridian Group plc, 1 Meridian Place, London EC1A 2BB
- **Directly to line manager or HR:** For less sensitive matters where the reporter is comfortable self-identifying

Anonymous reports are fully accepted. Reporters should provide as much detail as possible (dates, names, documents), but anonymity will not affect the investigation.

### 4.3 Response and Investigation Process

Reports received via EthicsLine or email will be **acknowledged within 5 business days**. The Chief Compliance Officer (**Rachel Douglas**) assigns an investigation manager who is independent of the subject of the report. Investigations are completed within **30 business days**, although complex cases may take longer with the reporter kept informed of progress.

Findings are reported to the Audit Committee. Where criminal activity is discovered, the matter is referred to law enforcement.

### 4.4 Non-Retaliation

Meridian strictly prohibits retaliation against anyone who raises a concern in good faith. Any employee found to have retaliated against a whistleblower is subject to disciplinary action, up to and including termination. Reports of retaliation should be directed to the Chief Compliance Officer or the Group General Counsel.

## 5. Internal Audit and Monitoring

### 5.1 Internal Audit Function

The Internal Audit function provides independent, objective assurance on the adequacy and effectiveness of the Group's risk management, control, and governance processes. The **Internal Audit Director is Andrew Stavros**, who reports functionally to the Audit Committee and administratively to the CFO.

The annual Internal Audit Plan is risk-based, approved by the Audit Committee in January, and covers financial controls, IT systems, operational processes, and regulatory compliance. Each audit concludes with a report rating controls as Satisfactory, Needs Improvement, or Unsatisfactory.

### 5.2 External Audit

The Group's external auditor is **Deloitte LLP**, appointed for a 3-year term expiring at the FY2025 Annual General Meeting. Audit fees for FY2023 were $6.2 million. The Audit Committee evaluates auditor independence annually and reviews non-audit services to ensure they do not compromise auditor objectivity.

### 5.3 Audit Committee

The Audit Committee meets **quarterly** and comprises three independent non-executive directors, chaired by **Dame Patricia Hollis**. Key responsibilities include oversight of financial reporting, review of internal and external audit findings, and monitoring of the Group's whistleblowing arrangements.

Material weaknesses or significant deficiencies identified in internal controls are reported to the full Board within **5 business days** of identification. The Audit Committee has the authority to commission special investigations when necessary.
